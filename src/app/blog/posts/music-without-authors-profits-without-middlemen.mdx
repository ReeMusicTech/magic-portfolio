They say that every day, between 50,000 and 60,000 songs created by artificial intelligence are uploaded to streaming platforms. It is an obscene figure, don't you think? It is as if the entire world had agreed to hum at the same time, but without vocal cords. Some reports even suggest that one in every three songs we hear today is the child of an algorithm.

However, here comes the drama, the Greek tragedy of our time: the law states that copyright only protects what is born from a human brain. If you ask an AI to create a "romantic instrumental jazz" through a prompt and do not lift another finger, that work is not yours; it belongs to no one, or perhaps to the void. We are in what experts call the "Gray Zone".

But, my friends, the gray zone is the favorite place for the bold. Performance Rights Organizations (those famous PROs), those white elephants that manage copyrights, refuse to register these works because they do not see a human sweating in front of a piano. And what happens then? Well, the front door swings wide open. If the PROs do not want to manage AI-generated music, then you owe them absolutely nothing.

This is where this reflection begins. If developing software is easier today than ever and creating music is child's play with AI, the real masterstroke is total independence. It is music that can be monetized directly, without intermediaries taking the lion's share.

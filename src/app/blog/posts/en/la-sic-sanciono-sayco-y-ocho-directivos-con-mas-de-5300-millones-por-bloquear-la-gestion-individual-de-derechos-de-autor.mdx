This recent sanction against Sayco for abusing its dominant position validates a fight I began years ago, when I realized that royalties were not reaching artists fairly and transparently.

To address this problem, I developed Bido, a technology platform that allows businesses to access licensed music quickly and legally. At the time, we envisioned two key features: knowing in real time whether their stores were using Bido, and being able to generate a playlist in less than 30 seconds. However, when we tried to obtain catalogs to validate our model, the major publishers refused to let us use their repertoire, which forced us to change strategy and turn to independent artists.

There we faced two major challenges: first, not all music is suitable for in-store programming, and second, the resulting reduction of our catalog. What made us proudest back then were the results we were achieving in monetization for musicians, which led many artists to approach us to manage their intellectual property on our platform. Some of them were members of ASCAP and others of Sayco. We had no problem with the former, but those affiliated with Sayco were investigated by their society for wanting to work with us. Realizing this would make it harder to build our catalog, we decided to file a formal complaint in 2020. Today, the SIC confirms that those practices limited competition and harmed creators.

One key aspect of Resolution 90629 of 2025 particularly caught my attention, specifically section 12.3.1, where the Superintendence ORDERS Sayco to establish and implement a compliance program on free economic competition, aligned with the NTC 6378 of 2020 certification and the SIC's Guidance Manual, which call for three years of monitoring. Most significantly, the Superintendence analyzed compliance recommendations from several countries and international organizations, including the OECD, to define the essential elements of these programs. This international approach not only validates the seriousness of the process, but also opens an important door for tech ventures like ours. The fact that Colombia is adopting global standards, such as those of the OECD, makes me think about the possibility of expanding into other markets where these best practices are already well established. Seeing that our case and the solution we propose are aligned with global trends reinforces our vision and motivates us to keep innovating.

From my point of view, the solution to these structural problems in the music industry lies in technology. This historic sanction by the SIC should serve as a catalyst for more tech-based entrepreneurs to develop solutions that, above all, benefit creators. Transparency, efficiency, and free competition are not just legal concepts; they are the foundation for building a fairer and more dynamic industry. At Bido, we will keep working so that artists regain control over their rights and so that technology becomes the bridge connecting their talent with the opportunities they deserve.
